WHEN TO USE: Before any call or message aimed at lowering a recurring bill — cable, internet, mobile, insurance, gym, alarm, satellite radio, storage.
Also use when a promo rate just expired, a renewal notice arrived, or the household asks "can we get this cheaper?"

# Bill negotiation and retention playbooks

## The one law of this skill

Front-line reps cannot give you the good price. Retention (a.k.a. "loyalty",
"customer solutions", "account review") can. Everything below is about reaching
retention with leverage in hand and a number already on the table.

## Prep: 15 minutes that decide the outcome

1. **Pull the actual numbers first.** Current monthly charge, what it was 13
   months ago, the promo end date, contract end date, early-termination fee
   (ETF), and total spend to date. In this app: `search_transactions` for the
   merchant, `recurring_and_bills` for cadence, the bill PDF in the vault if
   there is one.
2. **Find the competing offer.** The named competitor, their exact advertised
   price, speed/coverage tier, and term. "Somebody cheaper" loses. "Spectrum is
   at $49.99 for 500 meg, 24 months, in my zip" wins.
3. **Set three numbers before dialing:** your *anchor* (aggressive but sane —
   usually the new-customer promo price), your *target* (what you actually
   expect: 15–30% off), and your *walk-away* (the number below which you
   actually cancel). Never negotiate without a real walk-away.
4. **Know your account leverage:** tenure, autopay, paperless, bundled lines,
   never-late payment history, and whether you own your modem/equipment.

## The call structure

**Reach a human.** Say "cancel service" or "disconnect" at the IVR — that is
the retention routing keyword at nearly every carrier. If you land on a general
rep, ask once: *"Are you able to apply retention pricing, or should I be with
the cancellation department?"*

**Open warm, anchor hard, then stop talking.**

> "Hi — I've been a customer since 2019 and I'd like to stay. My bill went from
> $79 to $134 when the promo ended. Spectrum is offering me 500 meg at $49.99
> for two years, three miles from here. I'd rather not switch. What can you do
> to get me near that number?"

Then say nothing. Silence is the highest-yield tactic in this entire document.
The first person to fill it concedes.

**Never accept the first offer.** The first offer is scripted and is typically
40–60% of the discount the rep is authorized to give. Response:

> "I appreciate that, but $12 off doesn't close the gap. I need to be under
> $70 all-in to justify staying. Can you check what promotions I qualify for,
> including new-customer pricing?"

**Ask for the specific lever, not "a discount".** Reps grant catalog items:
loyalty credit, promotional pricing re-application, equipment fee waiver,
tier upgrade at current price, one-time bill credit, autopay/paperless
discount, price-lock. Name them one at a time.

**Trade, don't beg.** Offer autopay enrollment, paperless, a 12-month
commitment, or dropping a channel tier you never watch — in exchange for the
number. Reps have approval codes that unlock on commitment.

**Escalate once, politely, with a reason.**

> "I understand that's your limit. Could you connect me to someone in
> retention/loyalty who can approve more? I'm trying to avoid porting out."

Escalation works because the discount authority genuinely sits one level up.
Two escalations in one call is usually the ceiling.

**Close by locking the record.** Get: new monthly total *including fees and
taxes*, promo duration and end date, whether a contract or ETF is created,
confirmation number, and the rep's name/ID. Then: *"Can you email me written
confirmation of this today?"* Save that email to the vault. Undocumented
verbal discounts evaporate at the next billing cycle — this is the single most
common failure mode.

**Hang up and redial if you get a "no".** Different rep, different authority,
different mood. Three attempts across three days is normal, not obsessive.

## Cancel-to-win timing

The credible cancellation threat is a *scheduled* one, not a threatened one.
Set a disconnect date 10–20 days out. Systems flag pending disconnects and
"win-back" desks call with offers that front-line retention cannot match. Rules:

- Never schedule a disconnect you are not willing to let happen.
- Confirm portability first (mobile: get your account number + transfer PIN
  before you threaten anything).
- Check the ETF math: if the ETF exceeds 6–8 months of the savings, negotiate
  instead of leaving, or ask the *new* provider to buy out the ETF — many will.
- Best calendar windows: the last 3 days of a month/quarter (retention quotas),
  and the 30 days before a promo or contract expiration.

## Category-specific notes

**Cable / internet / TV.** Highest success rate of any category — 60–80% get
something. Ask specifically for "new customer promotional pricing applied to my
account". Return leased equipment and buy your own modem/router: typically
$10–$18/month recovered permanently. Broadcast TV and Regional Sports fees are
usually non-negotiable line items; get them offset with a credit instead.

**Mobile.** Threaten porting, not cancelling. Ask about switching to a current
plan generation (older plans quietly cost more), autopay discounts, and
multi-line pricing. Consider the MVNO on the same network as a real walk-away.

**Auto and home insurance.** Do not call your carrier first — shop 3+ quotes
with identical coverage limits and deductibles, then take the best competing
quote to your agent and ask them to match or explain the gap. Ask explicitly
about: multi-policy bundle, raising deductibles, telematics/safe-driver,
low-mileage, paid-in-full, alarm/water-shutoff/roof-age credits, and any
affinity discount (employer, alumni, professional group). Re-shop every 2 years
minimum — loyalty is priced *against* you in most states. Never lower liability
limits to save money; raise deductibles instead.

**Gyms and clubs.** Lowest phone success, highest in-person success. Go to the
front desk at month-end, ask for the current promo rate, and ask them to waive
the annual "maintenance fee". Freeze/hold options are usually free but never
advertised.

**Medical bills.** Different game: request an itemized bill (CPT-coded), check
for duplicates, ask for the self-pay/prompt-pay rate, request financial
assistance/charity care (nonprofit hospitals are required to have a policy),
and negotiate a 0% payment plan. Never put a hospital bill on a credit card
before doing this.

## Reporting back to the household

Always state: old price, new price, monthly and annualized savings, promo end
date, and whether a new contract or ETF was created. Then set a reminder for 30
days before the promo expires — the renegotiation calendar is where the real
compounding happens.
